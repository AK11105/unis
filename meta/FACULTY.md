## Candidate Research Profile

The candidate's core academic interest is in:

- Artificial Intelligence (AI)
- Machine Learning (ML)
- Deep Learning
- Representation Learning
- Related areas of modern AI/ML

### Specific Current Research Interest

The candidate has recently developed a **specific interest in World Models and Representation Learning**.

This should be treated as an important positive signal when evaluating faculty research fit.

World Models should NOT be treated as the only acceptable research direction. The candidate remains broadly interested in AI/ML and related fields.

The desired interpretation is:

> **Broad core interest: AI / ML and related fields**
>
> **Specific emerging/high-interest direction: World Models + Representation Learning**

When researching faculty at a university, identify faculty whose current research genuinely overlaps with the candidate's interests.

Prioritize faculty working in:

### Highest Priority

- World Models
- Learned world models
- Model-based learning
- Representation learning
- Self-supervised representation learning
- Latent representations
- Learning structured representations
- Predictive representations
- Generative world models
- Video/world modelling
- Embodied AI involving learned world models
- Agent learning involving world models
- Planning and reasoning with learned models

### Very High Priority

- Deep learning
- Generative AI
- Generative models
- Reinforcement learning
- Model-based reinforcement learning
- Sequential decision making
- Embodied AI
- Computer vision
- Video understanding
- Multimodal learning
- Causal representation learning
- Self-supervised learning
- Foundation models

### Broad Relevant Areas

Also consider faculty working in:

- AI
- Machine learning
- Computer vision
- NLP
- robotics
- probabilistic machine learning
- statistical learning
- computational neuroscience where directly relevant to representation learning or learning agents

Do not force a connection where none exists.

# World Models Matching

World Models should receive additional weight during faculty matching.

A faculty member does NOT need to explicitly use the phrase "world models" to be relevant.

Investigate whether their research involves concepts such as:

- learning environment dynamics;
- latent dynamics;
- predictive models of environments;
- learned simulators;
- generative models of environments;
- video prediction;
- representation learning for agents;
- model-based RL;
- planning using learned models;
- embodied agents learning internal models;
- multimodal representations of environments;
- temporal/sequence representations;
- learning compact latent state representations.

However:

> **Do not label a faculty member as working on World Models merely because they work in AI, deep learning, reinforcement learning, or computer vision.**

There must be a defensible research connection.

---

# Representation Learning

Representation learning is a major research signal.

Give additional relevance to faculty whose work involves:

- self-supervised learning;
- unsupervised representation learning;
- latent-variable models;
- learned representations;
- multimodal representations;
- disentangled representations;
- hierarchical representations;
- temporal representations;
- visual representations;
- semantic representations;
- representation learning for agents.

Again, distinguish direct research involvement from broad topical overlap.

# Faculty Matching Evidence

For every faculty member selected as a relevant match, collect evidence from authoritative sources, preferably:

1. Official university faculty profile
2. Official laboratory/research-group page
3. Official research page
4. Recent publications
5. Official personal academic webpage

Prefer **current research activity** over historical work.

Where possible, prioritize recent publications/research directions rather than relying only on a generic faculty biography.

---

# Match Classification

Classify faculty matches using:

### Tier 1 — Direct Match

Strong direct overlap with:

- world models;
- representation learning;
- model-based learning;
- closely related research.

### Tier 2 — Strong Adjacent Match

Strong overlap with areas such as:

- generative modelling;
- reinforcement learning;
- computer vision;
- embodied AI;
- multimodal learning;
- self-supervised learning;

with a meaningful connection to world models or representation learning.

### Tier 3 — Relevant AI/ML Match

Clearly relevant to the candidate's broader AI/ML interests but without a strong specific connection to world models or representation learning.

### Do Not Include

Do not include faculty merely because:

- they work in "AI";
- they are in the CS department;
- they are highly famous;
- they have worked on ML historically;
- their work is vaguely computational.

Research relevance must be defensible.

---

# Research Recency

Prefer faculty whose relevant research is active/current.

When assessing a match:

- check their current faculty profile;
- examine recent publications/projects when available;
- distinguish current research from older work;
- do not infer current interests solely from a paper published many years ago.

If a faculty member previously worked in a relevant area but no longer appears active in it, record that distinction.

---

# Output For Each Faculty Match

For every selected faculty member, provide:

- Name
- Current position
- Department / research group
- Research areas
- Why they match the candidate
- Specific connection to World Models, Representation Learning, or broader AI/ML
- Evidence/source URLs
- Match tier: Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3

Keep the explanation factual and concise.

Example:

### Professor X — Tier 1

**Research:** representation learning, generative modelling, learned environment dynamics.

**Why relevant:** Direct overlap with the candidate's interest in world models and representation learning, particularly through research on learned latent representations and predictive environment models.

**Evidence:** [official faculty/research page]

---

# Important Constraints

Do NOT:

- rank faculty based purely on prestige;
- invent research connections;
- infer World Models expertise from generic AI terminology;
- treat every RL researcher as a World Models researcher;
- treat every generative-model researcher as a World Models researcher;
- treat every computer-vision researcher as a representation-learning researcher;
- rely on outdated research as evidence of current interests;
- change the candidate's broader AI/ML preference into an exclusive World Models preference.

The candidate is **broadly AI/ML-oriented**, with **World Models and Representation Learning as particularly preferred research directions**.

The final faculty list should therefore balance:

**direct World Models / Representation Learning fit**
+
**strong adjacent AI/ML research**
+
**broader relevant AI/ML faculty**

rather than searching exclusively for the words "world model".
